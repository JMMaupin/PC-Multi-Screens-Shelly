Locations for a PC that travels, profiles that switch screens only, a protected PC outlet, quick switches for accessories, and a taskbar kept auto-hidden.

Illustrated guide: **[jmmaupin.github.io/PC-Multi-Screens-Shelly](https://jmmaupin.github.io/PC-Multi-Screens-Shelly/)**.

## Install

Download `ShellyPCScreens-{version}.exe` and run it: it offers to update the installed copy, and asks for an administrator once. Running instances in every session close by themselves first.

The executable is not signed yet: on first launch, SmartScreen shows *Windows protected your PC* → *More info* → *Run anyway*.

## What's new

- **Locations.** A PC carried from one desk to another, with a power strip and screens of its own in the suitcase, now has a location per place: its devices, its PC outlet, its screens, its layout, its profiles. The application drives only the location the PC is at, recognised by that location's PC outlet drawing power; at none of them (its outlets draw nothing, or do not answer), it switches nothing, and the tray icon turns purple. Settings → Devices → *Location...* puts a device at a location, or creates one. Away from a desk, its power strips no longer slow anything down: devices are searched for in parallel at start and wake, and those of another location are left alone once the PC is located.
- **Profiles switch screens only.** Accessories (USB hubs, speakers, a lamp) and outlets whose type is not set stay as they are, whatever the profile; before, a profile cut every outlet it did not list. Accessories switched off by sleep still come back on wake, as they were.
- **Quick switches.** Tick *On/off switch in the quick menu* on an outlet (Settings → Outlets): it gets an on/off switch in the window of the profile shortcut. A click switches it at once; the window stays open.
- **The PC role is protected.** Ticking *Powers the PC itself* on another outlet used to take the protection away from the real one, and the next profile could cut the PC. The role now moves only to an outlet drawing like a running PC, and after a confirmation; the outlet losing it stays protected as *critical* while it still draws like a PC.
- **Keep the taskbar auto-hidden** (Settings → Outlets, *Windows options*): Windows sometimes turns auto-hide off when screens come and go; ticked, it is turned back on within seconds.
- **Which socket feeds which screen.** The screen map now shows, under each screen, the graphics card and the socket it is plugged into: *RTX 4080 SUPER · DP 2*, *HDMI 1*. With every cable in a cable tray, it is the way to find the one to unplug. The numbers follow the driver's order; which socket on the bracket is *DP 2*, Windows does not say: unplug one cable once to find out.
- **Devices with a fixed address are reached directly.** Resolving a `.local` name could take up to eleven seconds per request on some networks: reading three power strips then took over thirty, and the app took minutes to find them at start. At each connection the app now asks each device for its Wi-Fi settings; if every enabled network, backup included, is set to the same static address, the one in use, requests go straight to it (readings: 0.7 s instead of 30). On DHCP, the name stays the way in. The MAC that answers is checked at every reading, so another device that took the address is never mistaken for this one. Settings → Devices shows *(fixed address)* in *Reached via*.
- **Delete an empty location.** A location emptied by moving its devices goes away by itself, except the first one, which stayed behind with its old layout once every device had moved elsewhere. Settings → Devices: select the location's row, then *Remove*. Only a location without any device can be deleted, and never the last one; its layout goes with it.
- **Devices found at start without help.** While the first connection was still looking for a device, the periodic refresh read it too, counted a failure and put it on hold for 15, then 30 s: once found, it still waited, and the PC stayed "at no known location" for a minute, as if a *Reconnect* were needed. A device being connected is now left to its connection, and read the moment it is found.
- **Which Wi-Fi network each device is on.** A new *Wi-Fi network* column in Settings → Devices, flagged *(backup)* when a device has fallen back to its backup network and stayed there: a restart makes it try its preferred one again.
- **A password change kept the PC outlet protected.** The device's connection, rebuilt after a password change, lost the list of outputs that must never be switched off; it now keeps it, like every other connection.
- **Layout capture with slow devices.** When the power strips took long to answer, one reading of the outlets could outlast the capture's 20 s, and the capture failed with *Windows is still arranging the screens* although everything was in place. A clean reading now always gets its confirming one. The reason of a refused capture also no longer stays under the map once it no longer holds (the PC found its location, every screen is back).
- **Screens plugged into another output are followed.** Windows tells screens apart by the graphics card output they use: once the PC had been moved and its cables put back in other outputs, the layout capture refused to work (*on but Windows does not see its screen*, *Unknown screen connected*) and every profile waited 20 s for screens that had in fact come back. A screen found again on another output is now relinked by itself, recognised by its model, and by its serial number when it gives one. Two identical screens swapped still need *Identify displays*. Settings then offers to save the new links (administrator).

## Check after updating

- **The PC outlet must draw above the off threshold while the PC runs** (Settings → PC power). The application now relies on it to know the PC is there: below it, it believes the PC is elsewhere and switches nothing.
- **Profiles only switch outlets whose type is *Screen*.** If one of your screens is still *Not set* in Settings → Outlets, set it to *Screen*: until then, profiles leave it alone.

With a single location, the hardware configuration is left as it was: the update does not ask for an administrator to save it.
